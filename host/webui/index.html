    Identify sweep

    Nothing here is required to design lighting. Both strips' wiring order is confirmed and ships as the default — per-key strip index 0 is the bottom-right key, and the chain snakes upward from there. This panel is for confirming that on your own unit, or correcting it: it lights one LED at a time on the pad, you click the position that lit, and Write mapping to config stores the result as an explicit layout.key_positions / layout.underglow_positions override.

    The table starts from the mapping in force, so an unchanged sweep just re-states the default. Positions are the pad's physical geometry: keys are [row, ordinal-within-row] left to right (not the scan-matrix column, and not the 4×4 grid column — the grid column is derived from it), underglow is [x, y] in the 3×3 grid, 0–2 each, centre excluded. Whether the matrix column order runs left to right is a separate question that only matters for input events (see docs/HARDWARE.md).

      On-screen cheat sheet

      A translucent panel in the corner of the screen showing what every control is bound to right now — thirteen unlabelled keycaps is the pad's one real ergonomic problem. It is drawn by the native lmhud helper, floats above full-screen apps without taking focus, and re-renders itself whenever the mode or profile changes. Drag it by its background to move it, close it with the × in its corner; where you put it is remembered. Bind cheat_sheet to a key to toggle it from the pad, or cheat_sheet_show on hold plus cheat_sheet_hide on release for a peek-while-held. Needs no device and no firmware.

      —

        Revert to stock firmware

        Work Louder's Input app already recognises this pad as a flashable board — the ESP32-S3's USB-Serial-JTAG console reports Espressif's VID and manufacturer string, which is exactly what the app scans serial ports for, and it drives the chip into download mode itself over DTR/RTS. Nothing has to be primed on the device. The only obstacle is this daemon, which holds the serial port and re-opens it every two seconds.

        Release blanks the pad, drops the port and stops reconnecting. Then open Input and use “Found device in bootloader mode, click here to reflash”. The flash writes at offset 0 without a full erase, so BLE pairing and the vendor keymap survive. Nothing here is destructive on its own and it is reversible — the LibreMicro image can be re-flashed the same way (docs/RECOVERY.md).
